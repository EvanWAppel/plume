## 1. Executive Summary

`plume` is a **members-based salon collective and community art space** opening in the **Cadence master-planned community in east Henderson, Nevada** — one of the fastest-growing residential areas in the country (top-5 best-selling master-planned community nationally, 2024–2026 [¹]). The salon combines two tiers of stylist space under one roof:

- **Front — open studio (≈8 stations):** flexible day-rate / part-time access for stylists building a book, testing independence, or working part-time. Low barrier to entry.
- **Back — private studios (≈6 suites):** monthly private rooms for established, independent stylists who want privacy, autonomy, and a lockable space.

Wrapped around both is a **cultural hub** — a rotating local-artist gallery (consignment), live music, and community/stylist events — that functions primarily as a **brand and foot-traffic engine** for a community that currently has no comparable gathering place.

**Why now / why here:** There is **no salon-suite operator in Cadence's ZIP codes (89011/89015)** [²]; the nearest are 8–12 miles away. Cadence's trade area is meaningfully **higher-income (median household income ≈ $93K in 89011 vs. ≈ $76–80K county-wide)** and still building out to ~12,250 homes through ~2029–2031 [³]. Getting established before the trade area saturates is the core strategic thesis.

**The founder is the model's biggest asset:** Rory is a **16-year licensed cosmetologist and color specialist**, a former Great Clips manager (systems/throughput experience), a **current salon-suite tenant** (she has lived the exact experience she'll be selling), and she brings a **portable, loyal client book** that anchors day-one revenue.

**The two make-or-break issues this plan confronts head-on:**
1. **Financing structure (§3):** the SBA generally treats pure *space-rental* businesses as ineligible "passive" businesses. The model is therefore structured as an **active, membership-and-services** business, and eligibility must be validated with an SBA lender / the Nevada SBDC before applying.
2. **Build-out cost discipline (§8, §12):** at ~14 units, a ground-up build-out ($500K–$900K) creates debt service this scale struggles to carry. The plan targets a **second-generation (former-salon) space** to slash build-out and protect the economics.

## 2. Concept & Vision

### 2.1 The problem it solves
- **For stylists:** independence is booming, but the options are lonely private suites (isolating, and often only available in month-long commitments far from Cadence) or old-model commission chairs (giving up 40–60% of revenue). There is no flexible, community-oriented, *local* option in Cadence.
- **For the community:** Cadence is a brand-new community with homes but few "third places" — somewhere to gather, see local art, hear live music, and feel a local identity. `plume` aims to be a small cultural anchor, not just a salon.

### 2.2 The concept in one line
> A local, members-based salon collective where stylists at every stage — from part-time day-renters up front to established pros in private studios in back — keep what they earn, run their own businesses, and belong to a space that doubles as Cadence's living room.

### 2.3 The two-tier journey (the differentiator)
The front and back are deliberately a **ladder**, not just two products:
- A stylist starts up front on **flexible day access** (low commitment, low cost) while building a book.
- As they grow, they **graduate to a private studio in back** (privacy, premium, monthly).
- Rory, as owner and working colorist, is the mentor/anchor at the center of it.

This "grow-with-us" pipeline is a retention engine most suite operators don't have, and it's a genuine story for lenders/investors. _`[RORY]` confirm this ladder framing matches your intent — see [Q6](#decisions--open-questions-for-rory)._

### 2.4 The cultural hub
Positioned as a **community & marketing engine** (a "side thing to inspire community"), not a core profit center:
- **Art:** rotating local-artist wall, sold on **consignment** (salon takes a commission), curated with Rory's approval; open to community submissions but not open-wall-anything.
- **Events:** live music, stylist-community meetups/parties, small gatherings — roughly monthly.
- **Role:** drives foot traffic, social content, and word-of-mouth that fills chairs and suites; contributes light ancillary revenue (consignment commission, occasional ticketed/sponsored events).

## 3. ⭐ Decision #1 — Financing structure & the SBA "passive business" problem

**This is the most important decision in the plan and it gates everything financial.**

### 3.1 The finding
SBA rules (SOP 50 10 8, effective 2025-06-01) make businesses whose income comes primarily from **renting space to independent operators** an **ineligible "passive business."** The rulebook **specifically names salon suites** _"where income is generated by renting space to accommodate independent businesses."_ [⁴] A plain rent (or rent-plus-dues) model, as originally described, likely **does not qualify for an SBA loan.**

### 3.2 What this does *not* mean
- It does **not** mean the business can't exist.
- It does **not** mean Rory can't borrow — **conventional bank loans and equipment financing are not bound by the SBA passive rule.**
- It does **not** apply to the parts of the business that are genuinely *active*: Rory's own color services, retail, education, and events.

### 3.3 The three paths

| Path | What it means | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Restructure as an active membership/services business** (recommended to preserve SBA access) | Sell **memberships** that bundle access + amenities + services (booking tools, marketing, back-bar, laundry, education, community), rather than bare assigned-space leases. Emphasize Rory's active service revenue, retail, and events. | Requires careful structuring & lender sign-off; the assigned private studios are the "riskiest" element for eligibility and may need to be framed/limited. |
| **B. Conventional financing** | Skip SBA; use a bank term loan and/or equipment financing. | Typically higher rate, larger down payment, shorter term, harder for a first-time borrower with no operating history. |
| **C. Hybrid / staged** | Launch lean on conventional or microloan (SBA Microloan ≤$50K is *not* subject to the same passive test in the same way — **verify**), prove the model, then refinance. | Slower; smaller initial footprint. |

### 3.4 Action before the financial model is finalized
**Validate the structure with a free Nevada SBDC advisor or an SBA-preferred lender** (see §17 resources) **before** committing to numbers. This is logged in `BLOCKED.md` as a 🔴 item because it gates the loan path. _`[RORY]` + Evan action._

> **How the rest of this plan handles it:** the business model in §7 is written in the **active membership/services framing (Path A)** by default, with rent-equivalent economics preserved underneath, so the plan reads as loan-eligible while we get it validated.

### 8.2 Build-out — the cost lever that makes or breaks the economics
- Salon tenant-improvement runs **$120–$200/sq ft** ($100 basic, $250–300 luxe); **plumbing is the #1 driver** (~$500–$1,500 per shampoo bowl); per private suite ~$17K–$35K to build [⁸].

### 8.3 Recommendation — target second-generation salon space
The single most powerful cost lever is leasing a **former salon** ("2nd-gen") that already has plumbing, stations, and ventilation. This can cut build-out by a large fraction and is the difference between tight and comfortable. **Priority in the site search.** ✅ **Answered (2026-09-15): yes — Rory is open to buying out / taking over an existing salon.** This unlocks the low-build-out path the whole financial model depends on. — [Q14](#decisions--open-questions-for-rory).

## 9. Operations

### 9.1 Self-service model (zero employees)
- **No staff.** Members run their own chairs, book their own clients, and handle their own supplies. The business provides the space, systems, and community.
- Implications: robust **access control** (keyed/app entry, esp. for back studios with 24/7 access), **cleaning** handled by member agreements + a periodic contracted cleaner, and **software** doing the administrative heavy lifting (billing, access, front-chair reservations).

### 9.2 Booking / management software — build vs. buy (a deliverable for Rory)
The operator's real software needs are narrower than a full salon: **(a) membership/rent billing (recurring), (b) front open-studio day-chair reservations, (c) access control, (d) a light community/marketing layer.** Individual stylists bring their own client-booking tools.

| Option | ~Cost | Strengths | Weaknesses | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vagaro** | ~$30/mo base + per-user | Broad; has rental/booth features, marketplace, payments | Per-user costs add up; built for salons not pure suites | Good off-the-shelf default |
| **GlossGenius** | ~$24–48/mo | Beautiful, simple, integrated payments | Solo-stylist focused, weaker multi-tenant/rental mgmt | Better for individual stylists than the operator |
| **Boulevard** | ~$195+/mo | Premium, powerful | Expensive; overkill for self-service suites | Poor fit |
| **Meevo (Millennium)** | Quote-based, higher | Enterprise-grade, robust | Complex, costly for 14 units | Overkill |
| **Suite-specific tools** (e.g., salon-suite mgmt SaaS) | Varies | Purpose-built for rent/membership + access | Smaller ecosystems | Worth shortlisting |
| **Custom build (Evan)** | Dev time + hosting + Stripe fees | Exactly fits the model; no per-seat fees at scale; owns the data; a differentiator | Build + maintenance burden; no vendor support; payments/compliance to handle | **Phase 2** — launch on off-the-shelf, build custom once the model is proven |

**Recommendation:** launch on an **off-the-shelf tool (Vagaro or a suite-specific SaaS)** to de-risk opening; treat the **custom build as a Phase-2 project** that narrowly targets front-chair reservations + membership billing. ✅ **Answered (2026-09-15):** Rory currently uses **Square** and is open to a salon-specific POS — she deferred the choice to us ("I don't have a clue. This is why I need you."). **Implication:** Square can bridge day-one payments/retail; the operator-side membership-billing + access-control layer is where Evan's build or a suite SaaS adds value. — [Q7](#decisions--open-questions-for-rory).
