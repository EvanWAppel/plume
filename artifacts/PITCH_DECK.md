---

## Slide 3 — The problem
**Independent stylists have bad options. Cadence has none.**
- Old-model commission chairs take **40–60%** of a stylist's revenue
- Private suites are isolating and usually far away — **nearest is 8–12 miles from Cadence**
- Cadence has ~5,000+ homes and almost no "third place" — nowhere to gather

**Notes:** Two problems, one solution. Stylists want independence without isolation; the community wants an identity.

---

## Slide 4 — The solution
**Two tiers under one roof, plus a reason to walk in the door.**
- **Open Studio (front, ~8 stations):** day-rate / part-time — low barrier, keep 100% above a flat fee
- **Private Studios (back, ~6 suites):** monthly, private, all-inclusive
- **Cultural hub:** local-artist gallery (consignment), live music, stylist meetups
- **The ladder:** start up front → graduate to a private studio as you grow

**Notes:** Emphasize the ladder — it's a retention engine competitors don't have.

---

## Slide 5 — Why now, why here
**Cadence is one of the fastest-growing communities in America — and it's wide open.**
- **Top-5 best-selling master-planned community in the U.S.** (2024–2026)
- Growing from ~5,200 homes toward **~12,250 by ~2029–2031**
- **No salon-suite operator exists in Cadence's ZIP codes** yet
- First mover builds the local brand before a franchise arrives

**Notes:** This is the urgency. The window is open *now*; it won't stay open.

## Slide 6 — The market
**A higher-income, homeowner-heavy community with room to grow.**
- Median household income **~$93K** (89011) vs. ~$76–80K county-wide
- ~78% owner-occupied; median age ~44
- Retail anchored by Smith's Marketplace, EOS Fitness, Ulta on the Lake Mead corridor
- ~1,600 hairstylists in the metro to recruit from

**Notes:** Money in the community, stylists to fill the chairs. Both sides of the marketplace are here.
